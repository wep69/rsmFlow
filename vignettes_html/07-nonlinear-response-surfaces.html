Two-factor nonlinear response surfaces and GA initialization

Scope

rsm_nonlinear_fit() is intended for two quantitative factors when a scientifically meaningful nonlinear surface is preferable to a polynomial approximation. Built-in structures are listed by:

rsm_nonlinear_models()
#>                   model    parameters
#> 1 mitscherlich2_product       A,k1,k2
#> 2  mitscherlich2_offset A,k1,k2,b1,b2
#> 3     gompertz2_product A,b1,k1,b2,k2
#> 4     michaelis_menten2       A,K1,K2
#> 5           asymptotic2 A,B1,k1,B2,k2
#> 6                hoerl2 A,b1,c1,b2,c2
#> 7       linear_plateau2 A,s1,t1,s2,t2
#> 8    quadratic_plateau2 A,c1,t1,c2,t2
#> 9                custom  user-defined
#>                                   interpretation
#> 1                      multiplicative saturation
#> 2         multiplicative saturation with offsets
#> 3             multiplicative Gompertz saturation
#> 4         two-factor Michaelis-Menten saturation
#> 5                   additive asymptotic response
#> 6                       two-factor Hoerl surface
#> 7           increasing two-factor linear plateau
#> 8 smooth increasing two-factor quadratic plateau
#> 9                       custom nonlinear formula

Parameter initialization is separated from final fitting. start="ga" searches declared parameter bounds by minimizing residual sum of squares, then passes the best parameter vector to nlsLM, nls, or gnls. This prevents GA from being confused with the final inferential estimator.

Example 1: Mitscherlich product with GA starting values

For positive factors, the built-in product form is

\[ \mu=A\{1-e^{-k_1x_1}\}\{1-e^{-k_2x_2}\}. \]

set.seed(201)
d <- expand.grid(N=seq(10,180,length.out=8),P=seq(5,100,length.out=7))
d$Yield <- 12*(1-exp(-.025*d$N))*(1-exp(-.04*d$P)) + rnorm(nrow(d),0,.12)
fit_m <- rsm_nonlinear_fit(d,"Yield",c("N","P"),model="mitscherlich2_product",
  start="ga",start_control=list(popSize=35,maxiter=80,run=25),engine="nls")

Example 3: custom nonlinear model

A custom formula is allowed when parameter bounds or explicit starts are supplied.

custom_formula <- Yield ~ A*(1-exp(-kN*N))*exp(-qP*(P-P0)^2)
fit_custom <- rsm_nonlinear_fit(d,"Yield",c("N","P"),model="custom",
  formula=custom_formula,
  start=c(A=12,kN=.02,qP=.0002,P0=60),
  engine="nls")
rsm_plot(fit_custom,"contour")
#> Ignoring unknown labels:
#> • fill : "Yield"

Parameter identifiability

Nonlinear convergence alone is not sufficient evidence that parameter estimates are stable. rsm_nonlinear_diagnostics() reports the singular values and condition number of a numerical prediction Jacobian, parameter correlations when a covariance matrix is available, convergence status, fit measures, and the initial-value search engine.

Generalized nonlinear least squares

Tier 3 includes nlme::gnls() for nonlinear responses with unequal variance and/or correlated errors. Variance structures are explicit rather than automatically inferred.

fit_gnls <- rsm_nonlinear_fit(d,"Uptake",c("N","P"),model="michaelis_menten2",
  start=c(A=15,K1=35,K2=22),engine="gnls",variance="power")

Optimum uncertainty and economics

unc <- rsm_optimum_ci(fit_mm,B=999,method="parametric")

For ordinary homoscedastic `nls`/`nlsLM` fits, this parametric workflow simulates Gaussian response error, refits, and reoptimizes. For `gnls`, where fitted variance and correlation structures can be substantially richer, the current development version defaults to asymptotic coefficient simulation unless a future backend implements full structure-aware response simulation.
eco <- rsm_economic_optimum(fit_mm,response_price=1200,factor_cost=c(N=5.5,P=6.0))

For nonlinear surfaces the bootstrap refits the nonlinear model in each replicate and records failures. Failed convergence is therefore visible in B_valid and failures rather than being silently discarded from the audit trail.
